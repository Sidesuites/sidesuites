ADUs in Florida: the complete 2026 guide.

An accessory dwelling unit (ADU) is a second, smaller home built on the same lot as an existing single-family house. In Florida, ADUs are now one of the fastest legal paths to add housing, generate rental income, or house family without buying another piece of land. Here's how they work, what they cost, and where the law stands.

What is an ADU

The 30-second definition.

An accessory dwelling unit — also called a backyard cottage, granny flat, guest house, in-law suite, or casita — is a fully independent home that sits on the same parcel as a primary residence. It has its own kitchen, bathroom, and entrance. It can be detached (a standalone structure in the backyard), attached (an addition), or converted (a garage or basement). Sidesuite builds a detached backyard home: 407 sq ft (11′ × 37′) with 1 bedroom, 1 bath and a full kitchen, 15 weeks from order to installed.

Why Florida is the right market. Florida Statute 163.31771 lets cities and counties permit ADUs as an affordable-housing tool, and most major cities have adopted ADU ordinances. See our full legal breakdown.
The four questions

What people actually want to know.

1. How much does an ADU cost in Florida?

Built the usual way, a Florida ADU runs about $84,000 to $300,000+, depending on size, finishes, site work, and contractor. Sidesuite's answer: $99,999 for the finished home, appliances included, plus installation, which is billed separately and quoted for your lot (estimated around $30,000). Permits, site prep and utility tie-ins depend on your lot. Full breakdown on the ADU cost page.

2. How do you pay for one?

Most homeowners use a HELOC, cash-out refinance, or construction-to-perm loan. Some Florida credit unions and specialty lenders offer ADU-specific products that underwrite against future rental income rather than current equity. We track the active lenders on our financing page, and Sidesuite's own lender partners are opening soon.

3. Is it legal where I live?

Probably yes, but it depends on your city and county. Florida cities like Miami, Orlando, Tampa, and St. Petersburg have already opted in under Statute 163.31771. The Florida ADU laws page tracks the current rules and 2026 legislative history. For any address in Miami-Dade or Broward, Check My Lot answers instantly.

4. Can I rent it out?

In most Florida jurisdictions, yes — long-term rentals are typically permitted, short-term (Airbnb-style) rentals are sometimes restricted. A furnished one-bedroom backyard home rents long-term for $1,150–$2,200/month across Florida metros. Sidesuite comes unfurnished, so budget for furniture or expect lower rent unfurnished. What changed in 2026

The by-right bill didn't pass.

HB 313 and its Senate companion SB 48 would have required every Florida county and city to allow ADUs by right by December 1, 2026. SB 48 passed the Senate 38–0, but both bills died when the session ended on March 13, 2026. ADU rules stay local under §163.31771, so your city's or county's ordinance decides what your lot allows. The details are on the Florida ADU laws page, and Check My Lot answers for any address in Miami-Dade or Broward.
