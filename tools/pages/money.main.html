Do it in this order

  1. Confirm the lot can take the home. Unincorporated Miami-Dade: a lot of 7,500 sq ft or more inside the Urban Development Boundary, and a unit of 400 to 800 sq ft. On septic? Sewer usually comes first.
  2. Call the Property Appraiser before the tenant moves in. The rented unit comes off your homestead. Hiding it costs back taxes, a 50% penalty and 15% interest.
  3. Sign a written lease longer than six months, at market rent. Save three comparable listings that day. They protect your deductions.
  4. Hand your CPA an itemized invoice. Structure, appliances and site work on separate lines. The first-year write-off needs that split.
  5. Mark two dates. April 1: tangible property return (Form DR-405). April 15: Schedule E with your federal return.
Order matters. Call the Property Appraiser before the tenant moves in, and ask for the itemized invoice before you pay for the home.

Dates that move the numbers

Plain answers

What is an impact fee?

A one-time charge on new construction. It pays for roads, parks, schools, police and fire service. The impact fees line in your day-one number is our estimate; the published schedules for your place are shown beside it.

What is a write-off?

A cost the IRS lets you subtract from your rent before it taxes you. Loan interest, insurance, property tax and repairs all count. So does one slice of the cost every year for 27.5 years. That slice is called depreciation, and you spend nothing extra to get it.

Do I get that money back as a check?

No. A write-off shrinks your tax bill. It does not refund what you spent. At your 12% tax rate, every $1,000 in write-offs trims your tax by about $120.

What can shrink my number?

A home that sits empty. A big repair. Insurance or property tax that runs higher than our estimate. A higher loan rate. A rented home comes off your homestead exemption. A fee exemption comes with rent and tenant-income limits.

When you sell, the depreciation you took on the structure is taxed at up to 25%, and anything written off in year one at your regular rate.

How these numbers are figured

The method, line by line
  • The home has one price: $99,999 for the one-bedroom, $89,999 for the Efficiency. Installation and impact fees start as our estimates. Your site-plan quote replaces them.
  • Each place starts on the home its size rules allow: the Efficiency in the City of Miami, the one-bedroom everywhere else.
  • Impact fees start at $20,000, our estimate. What the published schedules print for your place is shown beside it, and is in no sum.
  • An affordable rental is priced with the impact fees off, and a parents' home with $10,000 less of them. Both wait on a yes from the county or the school board, both are marked Assumed, and each has a switch.
  • The loan has level payments for its whole term.
  • Rent and costs stay at today's level in every year.
  • Empty weeks come off the rent as a share of the year.
  • Property tax: the county adds the home at its market value on the January 1 after it is finished. We assume 80% of the home plus installation, our estimate, and the 2026 tax rate. A rented home gets no exemption. A family home joins the homestead under the $250,000 exemption that starts in 2028.
  • Income tax: the 2027 federal tax tables. Florida has no income tax.
  • Depreciation: one slice of the cost every year for 27.5 years. We count a full first year; a real one is a little less, counted from the month the home is ready to rent.
  • Rental losses up to $25,000 come off your wages. That shrinks above $100,000 of income and is gone at $150,000. The rest carries forward.
  • The first-year write-off is about 15% of the cost: appliances, furniture, fencing, walkways and a driveway.
  • Home office: the building over 39 years, the first year's loan interest and the running costs. What they save is your tax without them less your tax with them, on the 2027 tables: income tax after the 20% business income deduction, and self-employment tax. That tax is charged on 92.35% of profit, and its Social Security part stops at the wage base ($192,300 in 2027), so past about $208,000 of profit a deducted dollar saves only Medicare's 2.9% of it. We treat household income as one person's business profit.
  • What the property may be worth is shown three ways and is in none of the totals.
  • Every figure is rounded to the dollar, so a column can sit a dollar or two off its total.
  • Left out: selling the property, furniture, a tenant's utilities, HOA dues and rent increases.
