Moving out back and renting the main house takes the main house off your homestead: it loses the exemption and the 3% cap and is taxed on its market value from the next January 1. What the property may be worth

A permitted backyard home usually adds to what your property is worth. How much is an appraiser's call, and it is usually less than the home cost to build. The one local estimate we found, a 2025 survey of real estate professionals, puts a Miami ADU at about 35 cents of resale value for every dollar spent. A study of Los Angeles sales found homes with a permitted ADU sold for 7% to 9% more. Move the slider to your own estimate.

For a rental, what the home earns each month is a separate question from what it adds at resale.

The county also adds the home to your assessed value, which is what your property tax is figured on. That is not the same number as market value.

Do it in this order

  1. Confirm the lot can take the home. Unincorporated Miami-Dade: a lot of 7,500 sq ft or more inside the Urban Development Boundary, and a unit of 400 to 800 sq ft. On septic? Sewer usually comes first.
  2. Pick your tenant's income tier. The county exemption is for a tenant household at or under 120% of the area's median income. The fee exemption card below has the income and rent limits. The rent cap at that tier sits above market rent.
  3. Ask about the fee exemption before the permit. Email the Impact Fee Section and ask for the affordable housing exemption on one backyard home. It can exempt the county fees if it approves. Get the answer in writing.
  4. Call the Property Appraiser before the tenant moves in. The rented unit comes off your homestead. Hiding it costs back taxes, a 50% penalty and 15% interest.
  5. Sign a written lease longer than six months, at the full permitted rent. Save three comparable listings that day. They protect your deductions.
  6. Hand your CPA an itemized invoice. Structure, appliances and site work on separate lines. The first-year write-off needs that split.
  7. Mark two dates. April 1: tangible property return (Form DR-405). April 15: Schedule E with your federal return.
Order matters. Ask before the building permit. The school exemption is lost if you miss that point. The county exemption can still be claimed as a refund within one year of the certificate of occupancy.

Dates that move the numbers

Plain answers

What is an impact fee?

A one-time charge on new construction. It pays for roads, parks, schools, police and fire service. The fee lines in your day-one number are estimates from the published schedules.

What is a write-off?

A cost the IRS lets you subtract from your rent before it taxes you. Loan interest, insurance, property tax and repairs all count. So does one slice of the cost every year for 27.5 years. That slice is called depreciation, and you spend nothing extra to get it.

Do I get that money back as a check?

No. A write-off shrinks your tax bill. It does not refund what you spent. At your 12% tax rate, every $1,000 in write-offs trims your tax by about $120.

What can shrink my number?

A home that sits empty. A big repair. Insurance or property tax that runs higher than our estimate. A higher loan rate. A rented home comes off your homestead exemption. A fee exemption comes with rent and tenant-income limits.

When you sell, the depreciation you took on the structure is taxed at up to 25%, and anything written off in year one at your regular rate.

How these numbers are figured

The method, line by line
  • The home is $99,999. Installation and lot work start as our estimates. Your site-plan quote replaces them.
  • Fees come from the county schedules at the single-family rate, at the middle of the published range.
  • The loan has level payments for its whole term.
  • Rent and costs stay at today's level in every year.
  • Empty weeks come off the rent as a share of the year.
  • Property tax: the county adds the home at its market value on the January 1 after it is finished. We assume 80% of the build cost, our estimate, and the 2026 tax rate. A rented home gets no exemption. A family home joins the homestead under the $250,000 exemption that starts in 2028.
  • Income tax: the 2027 federal tax tables. Florida has no income tax.
  • Depreciation: one slice of the cost every year for 27.5 years. The first year's slice is smaller, counted from the month the home is ready to rent.
  • Rental losses up to $25,000 come off your wages. That shrinks above $100,000 of income and is gone at $150,000. The rest carries forward.
  • The first-year write-off is about 15% of the cost: appliances, furniture, fencing, walkways and a driveway.
  • The value estimate starts at 35% of the whole project cost, the one local survey figure we found. It is in none of the totals.
  • Every figure is rounded to the dollar, so a column can sit a dollar or two off its total.
  • Left out: selling the property, furniture, a tenant's utilities, HOA dues and rent increases.
