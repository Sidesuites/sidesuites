Palm Beach rules

How the county and cities handle ADUs.

Palm Beach County is a patchwork. The county itself permits ADUs in many unincorporated areas under its Workforce Housing program. Most of the major municipalities have their own ordinances:

  • West Palm Beach: ADUs permitted in single-family districts; 800 sq ft max.
  • Lake Worth Beach: ADUs permitted citywide; among the most permissive in the county.
  • Delray Beach: ADUs permitted in many residential districts.
  • Boynton Beach: ADUs permitted under the 2022 ordinance update.
  • Boca Raton: ADUs permitted, with the strictest rules and largest minimum lot size in the county.
  • Wellington, Jupiter, Palm Beach Gardens: permitted with varying conditions.
The common parameters: 900 sq ft max ADU size (varies), 5–10 ft setbacks, one off-street parking space, owner-occupancy required in some municipalities, long-term rental permitted everywhere, short-term rental usually restricted.

Palm Beach rental economics

Furnished 1-bedroom rents in West Palm Beach core, Lake Worth, and Delray sit at $1,750–$2,200/month. Boca Raton and the Town of Palm Beach push past $2,500. Among the highest gross yields of any Florida metro — and the workforce-housing rental tier the county actively promotes through its program. Sidesuite comes unfurnished, so budget for furniture or expect lower rent unfurnished.

Permit process

How to get an ADU permit in Palm Beach County.

  1. Identify your jurisdiction — county unincorporated, or one of the cities listed above. Each has its own portal.
  2. Zoning verification — confirm ADU eligibility for your specific parcel.
  3. Building permit application — drawings and a site plan for your parcel.
  4. Plan review + impact fee calculation — 4–8 weeks depending on city.
  5. Permit issuance + construction.
  6. Inspections + CO.

Palm Beach impact fees are among the highest in the state, but rents more than compensate. The home itself is 15 weeks from order to installed.
